# Forensic review: what the data says about the simulator (2026-11-03)

This file answers the review's request: reverse-engineer the data-generating process (DGP) before building another model.
It covers the evidence for each of the review's five areas and the audit run today, and it lists what is still unknown.
Section names refer to `experiments/RESULTS.md`. Dataset facts come from `DATASET.md` and `docs/dataset/numbers.json`.

## 1. The DGP as reconstructed from the data

The best supported model of one event:

```
P(click on candidate i | event e of user u) = exp(s_i) / (exp(s_0u) + sum_j exp(s_j))
s_i  = k * <t_u, v_i> + b_i + r * [i already in u's clicks]
t_u  = a direction set by the SET of u's clicked items (order, recency, timing irrelevant)
s_0u = a per-user no-click level (stable trait, no drift)
```

| Component | Evidence | Where |
|---|---|---|
| At most one click; a no-click outcome; 37% of events have no click | Every event has 10 distinct rows and 0 or 1 clicks | DATASET.md section 1; numbers.json |
| Taste is a set of items, one direction | Pooling: the cosine of the mean is best, the max is far worse; no multiple interests (k=2 users, attention); flat cosine across click lags in real data and in the replica | "Pooling in cosine space", "Posterior predictive checks", "Taste is a SET" |
| Item vectors live in about 8 isotropic dimensions, with varying norms | Eight eigenvalues at 0.089-0.097, then a noise floor; no clusters (k-means vs a matched Gaussian); no user archetypes | "The true taste space looks ~8-DIMENSIONAL", "No discrete categories", "User tastes are not drawn from archetypes" |
| Per-user propensity is strong and stable | 71% of per-user variance is real; no drift; no headroom even using the user's future click share | numbers.json; "Click propensity does not drift"; "Propensity has NO headroom" |
| Item appeal b_i is weak | Item CTR sd 0.019 vs 0.0144 by chance; corr(log impressions, CTR) 0.066 | numbers.json |
| Repeat bonus | Candidates already in the history: CTR 29% vs 5.7% (4.1x after the item's own rate); recent repeats favoured 1.3-1.6x | "How the simulator behaves", "Posterior predictive checks" |
| No skip, position, time-of-day or session effects | Shown-and-not-clicked 1.04x; CTR flat by file position; click share flat by hour and by time since the previous event | "How the simulator behaves", numbers.json, "No row-order artifact" |
| Static world | No item is first shown after day 0; no taste shift at the warm-up boundary; slow item drift | "No taste shift at the warm-up -> train boundary", DATASET.md section 4 |

**Replica test.** We generated data from this model, using our own vectors as the truth and real timestamps and slates,
and calibrated it to our MF's real AUC (`lab/replica_sim.py`). Our MF then behaves on the replica as it does on real
data. With an 8-dimensional truth (`lab/replica8_noise.py`):

- True history with true candidates scores 0.851.
- Estimated history with true candidates scores 0.843: the history side costs 0.008.
- True history with estimated candidates scores 0.820: **the candidate side costs 0.031**.
- Refitting each candidate's vector from its own impressions, even with the TRUE user tastes, adds nothing beyond the
  MF's prior.

The remaining error sits in item vectors estimated from about 140 impressions and 8 clicks per item. That is a data
limit, not a fitting limit.

**Unknown:** the exact taste construction (unit mean, sqrt-set or raw mean), the scale k and the propensity
distribution (measured: Beta-like, mean 0.63, sd 0.138). These affect the shape of the scores, and LightGBM and the
choice network learn that shape from data.

## 2. Candidate generation

| Question | Answer | Where |
|---|---|---|

| Does exposure track appeal? | Barely: corr(log impressions, CTR) 0.066; an item's CTR rises 5.9% to 6.9% across impression quintiles, already a feature | numbers.json; "Impressions as a signal of item appeal" |

## 3. The two problems: does the event get a click, and which candidate

- Global AUC = event level (0.68-0.69) x within event (0.835-0.845). Almost all row pairs are cross-event.
- **Ceiling.** With the TRUE per-user propensity alone, event-level AUC would be 0.668; ours is 0.684-0.692. The replica
  oracle with true tastes and propensity reaches 0.733-0.738 at event level. The gap between those two comes from taste
  error, not from a missing event mechanism.
- **Explicit event model, done twice:**
  - 26 Oct (`lab/two_stage.py`): event LightGBM with candidate-score spread, 0.69123 vs 0.68989; global +0.0003.
  - Today (`lab/forensic_audit.py`): event LightGBM on mean, max and min over the slate of all 169 features plus the
    model's event logit, cross-fitted by user. Event-level AUC 0.68392 vs 0.68419 (day 6) and 0.68514 vs 0.68485
    (day 5). The re-levelled global score is +0.00002 / -0.00008.
- **Slate attractiveness** is in the model as `*_lse` (log-sum-exp of each view over the slate) and as the choice
  network's no-click output. Event share P(click) x within-share decompositions are worse than the joint model
  (`lab/combine_check.py`).
- **Timing.** Within a user, click share rises with time since the last click (numbers.json: -0.008 under 30 min,
  +0.081 at 3-7 days). Today's audit shows the model reproduces it: actual / predicted is 0.94-1.06 in every
  gap x user-rate cell on both days.
- **Per-segment calibration** (offsets or blend weights by history length or gap, fitted on day 6) loses on days 5 and 4
  (`lab/segment_blend.py`).
- **No short-term mood.** The per-user residual shared by events of the same day (`lab/residuals.py`) does not reset at
  UTC midnight, and once the error of the estimated user level is removed, consecutive events share no residual at gaps
  under 8 hours (+0.004 to +0.008, se 0.004; `lab/mood_decay.py`). So the user's recent labelled events carry nothing
  for an evaluation event beyond the user's overall level.

## 5. Does local validation measure the right thing?

1. **Candidate policy:** reproduced (section 2).
2. **Metric:** the lab reports plain, eval-weighted (events reweighted to evaluation's mix of known clicks),
   gap-weighted (reweighted to evaluation's hours since the last click) and first-event AUC. All are global AUC over
   rows, including no-click events. Gap-weighted half B gave 0.82626 for the 40 model, vs 0.82566 public. Day 4 predicted
   the public change exactly once (40 vs 38: +0.00041 on both).
3. **Clustering:** intervals resample events. Today's audit compared resampling users: the widths are the same
   (gch vs binary, day 6: events [-0.00002, +0.00072], users [-0.00001, +0.00071]; about 1.95 day-T events per user).
4. **Distribution shift:** adversarial validation found one inconsistency (a decayed popularity counter), and fixing it
   is neutral. Every other feature matches between the lab and evaluation (per-feature AUC 0.48-0.52).
   "Day 6 up, day 5 down" results (today: the boosted choice model) are consistent with noise: the user-clustered
   intervals overlap zero on both days.

## The implied statistical problem, and where the error is

To rank rows globally, the model must estimate exp(s_i) / (exp(s_0u) + sum exp(s_j)) for each candidate:

- The per-user no-click level is at its ceiling.
- Repeats, timing and slate size are calibrated.
- The remaining error is in `<t_u, v_i>`:
  - Rare candidates: AUC 0.683 for 0-3 clicks (1.2% of rows), 0.778 for 4-10 (12.7%), 0.819 for 11-30 (47.7%),
    0.842 for 31+.
  - Light users: 0.75 with 1-5 known clicks.
- The replica says both are information limits of this data. Every unexplained part of the residual was checked:
  per item, residual variance equals binomial noise (1.01), and the per-user residual is a same-day "mood" that does
  not carry across days (`lab/residuals.py`).
